# Three questions on 1,200 applications

**Source.** Referral brings 10.7 hires per 100 applications, ahead of email (8.1), search (5.7), college (3.4) and social (2.3). It survives role adjustment, but not in every role: referral leads Design and Marketing; email leads Software, search leads Data, and college leads Operations. Referral and email are statistically tied (p = 0.53).

**Steps.** 48.4% of applicants who finish all four steps are shortlisted, against 9.3% of those who stop early: +39.1 points, about five times the rate. Every role shows a 35-49 point gap.

**Reply speed.** Paid roles reply in a median 3 days; unpaid, 7. 13.1% of paid applications never hear back, versus 29.6% of unpaid ones. The gap holds by month and for applications at least 21 days old, so censoring looks unlikely to explain it. It is large enough to act on.

**Recommendation.** Next month, pilot an automated nudge for applicants who stall before step four, with a holdout, and measure shortlisting. The association may reflect motivation or screening rules, so the pilot should test it.

**What I would not trust.** The 15 applications with missing source (excluded from source comparisons); blanks read as "never" when they may mean "not yet"; role cells built on 8-146 applications and at most 7 hires; any causal reading. Six duplicate rows removed; nine work-mode labels standardised.
